01 / Funnel Compression

Traditional consulting sells in 90 days. Ours sells in 14.

If the audit replaces the discovery call, and the deliverable replaces the proposal, the only thing left to sell is the next phase. The lawyer never decides on consulting. They decide on Phase 1, which is already paid for through the audit credit.

Compression isn't the strategy. It's the byproduct of selling artifacts instead of opinions.

Every unpaid hour a consulting firm spends on discovery, scoping, or proposal-writing gets baked into the price the next client pays. The audit-led model collapses that spend onto the prospect's invoice from day one. The audit is paid work. It is also the sales conversation. Both at once.

02 / Who Pays

Generic "law firms" don't buy operations work. High-volume contingency PI firms do.

The economic model that makes a $15K diagnostic feel cheap is contingency revenue running against $1M+ in annual marketing spend.

High-volume PI firms run on these proportions. Half the cost is acquiring a retained client. A third is processing the case. The rest is rent.

The buyer is not "lawyers." It's the managing partner of a firm spending real money on TV, paid search, paid social, and referrals, with no defensible answer to "what's our CAC by channel?" That partner is exposed in two places: marketing waste they can't measure, and case duration that compounds attorney cost. Both are leaks. Both are quantifiable. Both are recoverable.

03 / The Offer

$15K diagnostic. 100% credit. No proposal.

The audit is the proposal. The credit is the bridge. The lawyer never has to "decide on consulting" — only whether to proceed to Phase 1, which their own audit money already paid for.

"Worst case I pay $15K and get a great report. Best case I pay $15K and effectively pre-paid for $25K of work I was going to do anyway."

The credit mechanic does three things at once. It removes commitment friction. It justifies a higher diagnostic price than a discovery-priced product would carry. And it mirrors how lawyers already think about money — pay for the work, the work pays for itself.

04 / The Engine

Forge isn't memory. It's the funnel engine.

Every prospect interaction flows in. Every asset is assembled from the brain. Every funnel stage is observable. The brain is the operational layer, not the archive.

At each funnel stage, Forge does specific work. Content magnet: the brain assembles essays, podcast scripts, and video outlines from existing nodes; the Council pre-reviews voice and facts before publish. Diagnostic: auto-routes prospect signals into pre-qualified intake. The audit: assembles the deliverable from inputs, benchmarks, and Hansen patterns, then routes through Council. Retainer: assembles Phase 1 scope from audit findings.

The Council pattern is load-bearing for "no sales calls." Without it, agents shipping prospect comms is too risky. With it, the founder becomes a reviewer of pre-vetted artifacts — not an author of every word.

The forcing function for the agent layer is brain extraction maturity. The threshold: 70% extraction, 80% tagging, 60% enrichment answered. Until then, agents drafting prospect comms is dangerous. After that, the brain is a leveraged version of the founder. That maturity arc is the timeline to "no sales calls" — not a target date.

05 / The Year Ahead

Today the founder ships everything. In twelve months, the brain ships first drafts.

The arc isn't speed of revenue. It's compounding leverage. Each prospect interaction becomes a new node. Each shipped asset compounds the next.

HorizonState

Six monthsForge agents draft assets from brain context. Council pre-reviews voice + facts + risk. Founder approves single-card review.
Twelve monthsMost prospect interactions never need founder hands. Brain-to-action loop closes. Hüify scales without the founder as bottleneck.

The headline metric we'd display in the cockpit is agent-approval rate — the percentage of agent-proposed actions the founder approves without edits. When that climbs, the brain is becoming complete. When it stalls, it surfaces a gap to enrich.
